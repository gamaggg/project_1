# by.motion — movement

This skill governs HOW and WHEN elements move. Every frame is built by by-composition (layers A–C, formats, fonts, logo, two languages, "Style from practice"), the frame's visual style — by the preset and layers in by-presets, the character of motion — by the "Character by preset" table below. Load both skills before working. If a rule here conflicts with layer A of by-composition — layer A wins.

## Why motion — the main rule

Motion exists to **get the point across fast in pictures**: show text, a photo or an idea interactively so the viewer understands without reading long text. Motion explains, it doesn't decorate.
- **Start with the point.** Before the storyboard write in one sentence what the viewer must understand and how motion will show it: "before → after" — a transformation; "works anywhere" — the product stays still, the world changes; "3 steps" — steps appear one by one; "2× growth" — a bar grows; "what it's made of" — ingredients assemble into the product. A technique from the library is chosen for this sentence, not for beauty.
- **Picture instead of words.** If the thought can be shown (photo, number, diagram, product in use) — show it, cut the text to a caption. One thought per scene.
- **On topic.** Motion is tied to meaning and object: tea pours, cream spreads, money is counted, a message arrives. An effect unrelated to the topic — remove.
- **Smooth.** Motion is fluid and whole: eased curves (no jerks or linear stops), each move starts from the previous one, elements don't twitch or jump on the first frame, 30 fps without dropped frames. Sharpness (Bold, Street) is intentional and on the beat, not from being unfinished.
- **Test:** watch the video once without sound — the point is clear within the first 2–3 seconds and matches the sentence from the first item. If not — change the technique, not the details.

Formats: **animated Stories** and **graphic Reels** (no filming: text, photos, illustrations, infographics). Both 9:16, 1080×1920. Built in Claude Design and rendered straight to MP4.

## Workflow

0. **Memory and preset.** As steps 0–0a of by-composition: "Style from practice", preset and layers. No Design System — "Starting a new brand".
1. **Question before building**, if not clear from the request: duration (or how many Stories), whether there's a voice-over, whether the track should be baked into the file or added at publishing, whether it's for paid ads. In one message.
2. **Storyboard.** First line — the point in one sentence and how motion will show it ("Why motion"). Scene table: # · time (start–end) · on screen · text · movement · music accent. For a Stories series — a row per Story. Choose 1–2 techniques (in videos over 20 s — up to 3, see "Combining techniques") from the "Technique library" (M1–M20) for the whole video and name them in the storyboard. Show the user before animating: editing a table is cheaper than a video. If there's no one to ask — go on and attach the storyboard to the summary.
3. **Key frames.** Build each scene as a static artboard in the "everything in place" state by by-composition and by-presets and pass levels 1 and 1b. Motion won't fix a bad frame.
4. **Animation** — per "Time", "Movement", "Building in Claude Design".
5. **Music and rhythm** — per "Sound".
6. **Check** — per "Check".
7. **Delivery** — per "Delivery".

## Time

- **Reading time.** After text has fully appeared, it stays still for at least **1 s + 0.3 s per word** (Japanese/Chinese: 1 s + ~0.1 s per character; Korean: per word group), minimum 1.5 s. Count by the longer language. A large number or 1–3 words — minimum 1.2 s.
- **Amount per frame** — A1 limits for Stories (body text up to 25 words). Better less text and more scenes.
- **Hook in the first 1–2 s.** The main phrase or number reads within the first second; don't start with an empty background, a logo or a long intro.
- **Ending.** The last frame — a conclusion or CTA of up to 4 words and the logo (A6), held 1.5–2 s without movement.
- **Duration.** Graphic Reels — usually 7–20 s, longer only if the text requires it. Stories — 5–15 s each, a series of 3–7.
- **Text rhythm:** the headline appears before the body text; order of appearance = reading order (A2).

## Movement

### General rules
- **One movement per scene** and no more than 2–3 elements moving at once. The main thing moves — the rest waits.
- **One set for the whole video:** 2–3 enter/exit techniques, one set of easing curves and durations. Same set across a Stories series and a content pillar.
- **Curves:** enter — ease-out, exit — ease-in, transition — ease-in-out. Linear only for continuous motion (ticker, slow drift).
- **Durations:** element enter 300–600 ms, exit faster (200–400), scene change 300–500. Stagger between lines or elements 60–120 ms. The preset changes these numbers (table below).
- **Text appears by lines or words.** By letters — only a hero of 1–3 words. While text is being read it doesn't move (a barely noticeable drift of background or photo is OK, no more than 2–3% per scene).
- **Numbers** — a counter from 0 (or from "before") to the exact number in the text in 0.8–1.2 s, the final value held at least 1 s. A visualisation (ring, bars, line) draws in together with the counter and stops exactly at the value (A7).
- **Photos:** slow push-in or pan (scale 100→105–108% per scene), a wipe mask on enter. The main person's face doesn't leave the frame and isn't covered by moving text (A12).
- **No flashes:** no more than 3 sharp brightness changes per second, no flicker of contrasting colours — dangerous for some viewers.
- **Nothing "for liveliness":** no meaningless motion (spinning icons, bouncing arrows, background particles) unless the preset calls for it.

### Character by preset

The frame looks per the preset card in by-presets (colour, font, photo, graphics, layouts L); it moves per this table.

| Preset | Movement | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral, Corporate, Fintech | fade + rise 24–40 px, lines and charts draw in, number counters | calm, 400–600 ms |
| Editorial | wipe masks, text line by line from under a rule, slow push-in on photos | slow, 600–900 ms |
| Health | soft fade, scale 98→100%, diagrams draw in as lines | calm, 500–700 ms |
| Bold | hard cuts, full-frame kinetic typography, word by word on the beat | fast, 150–300 ms, cuts on the beat |
| Minimal | opacity only, long pauses, one element at a time | very slow, 700–1000 ms |
| Playful | bounce and overshoot, stickers "pop", gentle wobble | lively, 300–500 ms |
| Street | stop-motion (animation "on twos", 12 fps), jitter, layered collage; glitch — rarely | ragged, on the beat |
| Lifestyle | slow push-ins on photos, soft transitions through opacity | calm, 500–800 ms |

Layers: **Promo** — the price or percentage "punches in" on the strong beat, the offer deadline stays longest; **Cultural code** — the ornament draws in as a line or unfolds from the centre; **Holidays** — one festive device per video, no fireworks in every scene; other layers move by the base preset. The brand and "Style from practice" outrank the table.

## Formats

### Animated Stories
- Zones — as in static Stories: nothing important in the top 250 px and bottom 340 px, **including during motion** (text doesn't fly in from under the progress bar).
- Interactive (A10c): the sticker zone is free the whole time, motion doesn't cross it. The question appears and settles within the first 1.5 s, then the frame is calm.
- Series: one set of movements and one headline position; each Story makes sense on its own (they get swiped). The first frame of each Story already reads.
- A Story shorter than its reading time — no: duration = enter + reading time of all text + 0.5 s.

### Graphic Reels
- Zones — as for the Reels cover (A10b): text and faces in x 72–940, y 250–1500 for the whole video; ~420 px at the bottom for caption and audio, ~140 px on the right for buttons.
- Structure: hook (1–2 s) → 3–6 scenes, one thought each → ending with the logo. One transition type between scenes for the whole video.
- Cover — a separate static frame per A10b; best to take the hook scene in its final state.
- The video is understandable without sound. With a voice-over — **subtitles are mandatory**: up to 2 lines, up to ~32 characters per line (Japanese/Chinese ~13–16 full-width characters, Korean ~16–18), 48–56 px, in y 1150–1450, the brand's body font, contrast per A8 (a calm background-colour plate is OK). Change by phrase, in sync with the voice; word highlight — only if the preset is lively (Bold, Playful, Street).

### Two languages (A13)
- **Switch:** frame in the primary language → the same frame in the second language. Reading time for each language separately, equal (by the longer one). Only the text changes (opacity transition 300 ms or a wipe); background, photo and shared elements (numbers, dates, logo) stay.
- **Both on screen:** any mixing method from A13; the primary language appears first, the second follows 150–300 ms later.
- Subtitles with two languages: the voice-over language as the main line, the second language a line below, smaller and in a tone; no more than 3 lines together.

## Sound

The skill suggests a track and edits to the rhythm.
- **Track suggestion** — 2–3 options: mood, genre, tempo (BPM), from which second to take the fragment. By preset: Corporate/Fintech — calm electronica 90–110 BPM; Bold/Street — hip-hop, phonk, drum 120–150; Minimal/Editorial — ambient, piano 60–90; Playful — indie pop, ukulele 100–120; Lifestyle — lo-fi, soul 80–100. A trending sound — only if the user sent it or it's in the Trend Library; don't invent track names — describe the character and where to look.
- **Rights.** Better to add the track at publishing from Instagram's audio library. Business accounts and ads only have music without copyright restrictions — advise looking there. Bake into the file only a track licensed for commercial use (ask whether the client has one).
- **Editing to the beat.** From the tempo: beat = 60 / BPM seconds (120 BPM → 0.5 s, a 4-beat bar → 2 s). Scene changes on the start of a bar, accents (number, price, logo) on the strong beat. Scene length is a whole number of bars, **but not shorter than the reading time**: if it doesn't fit — take the next bar, don't cut reading.
- Mark tempo and bars in the storyboard; if the track will be added later — build to the chosen BPM so changes land on any track of that tempo.

## Building in Claude Design

- One 1080×1920 artboard per video or per Story; frame and margins from by-composition (`--safe-top: 250px; --safe-bottom: 340px` for Stories).
- **All timings in one place in the code:** a scenes object (start, duration, text) and variables for durations and curves. So timing is checked in the code and changed in one place.
- Animate transform and opacity (smooth), not width/top/left. All timings run off one timeline, not separate delays.
- Fonts — per A11: the whole video = one series; the font is loaded before the animation starts (otherwise the first frames render in a fallback font).
- Render straight to MP4 with Claude Design.

## Check

Modes and independent reviewers — as in by-composition. In addition:
- [ ] **The point:** the sentence "what the viewer must understand" is written in the storyboard; a reviewer without the brief retells it after one viewing without sound. Every movement is on topic; no jerks or jumps.
- [ ] **Key frames** passed levels 1–3 of by-composition (viewer test — on still frames and on the video).
- [ ] **Timing from the code:** for every text, still time ≥ 1 s + 0.3 s × words, or 0.1 s × characters for Japanese/Chinese (minimum 1.5 s); calculate and list violations in the summary.
- [ ] The hook reads within 2 s; the ending with the logo holds 1.5–2 s.
- [ ] **Zones throughout:** check frames every 0.5 s — text doesn't enter service zones or the sticker zone, the face isn't covered.
- [ ] One set of movements and curves; no more than 2–3 elements moving at once; the character matches the preset.
- [ ] Counters and visualisations stop exactly at the number in the text.
- [ ] No flashes more than 3 per second.
- [ ] Scene changes land on the bar of the chosen tempo; the video is understandable without sound; subtitles are in sync with the voice.
- [ ] **The final MP4** watched in full at 375 px width (as on a phone): correct duration, the brand fonts on the first frames, no jerks or black frames at start and end, everything readable on first viewing.

## Delivery

- **Video:** MP4 (H.264), 1080×1920, 30 fps (Street with stop-motion — also 30, with 12 fps motion inside), sRGB. Build straight to MP4 in Claude Design. Stories — a separate file each.
- **Names** — per by-composition "Delivery": `brand_pillar_YYYY-MM-DD_reel.mp4`, `_story01.mp4`, cover `_cover.png`.
- **In the summary:** the storyboard with final timings; 2–3 track options (mood, BPM, where to look) and from which second; which stickers to add to Stories; what didn't pass the check and why. After publishing — a line in the publishing log in `learned.md` (format "graphic Reels" / "animated Stories").

## Learning

Same as "Learning on the project" in by-composition: the user's edits on motion ("slower", "no bounce", "sharper cuts") are recorded in `learned.md` as a separate line "Motion: …" in the "Rules" section, with the user's OK. Reels statistics (retention, completion) are analysed per "What worked — by statistics"; a retention drop in the first 2 s is a signal to redo the hook.
